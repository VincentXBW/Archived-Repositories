What is Skin Cancer?

Skin cancer is the out-of-control growth of abnormal cells in the epidermis, the outermost skin layer, caused by unrepaired DNA damage that triggers mutations. These mutations lead the skin cells to multiply rapidly and form malignant tumors. The main types of skin cancer are basal cell carcinoma (BCC), squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) and Melanoma.

Types of Skin Cancer

1. Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC)

The most common type of skin cancer. It rarely spreads but can cause severe damage to surrounding tissues if left untreated. BCC is a type of skin cancer that starts in the basal cells, which are the deepest cells in the epidermis

  • Appears as a shiny, pearly bump or a pink growth.
  • Common on sun-exposed areas like the face and neck.
  • Caused by long-term sun exposure.

2. Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC)

Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) of the skin is the second most common form of skin cancer. It develops in the squamous cells of the epidermis, the outermost layer of skin. When caught early, most SCCs are curable.

  • Often looks like a scaly, red patch or a sore that won’t heal.
  • Common on sun-exposed areas such as the ears, face, and hands.
  • Can be aggressive if not treated early.

3. Melanoma

Malignant melanoma is a serious form of skin cancer that begins in cells known as melanocytes. While it is less common than basal cell carcinoma (BCC) and squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), this skin cancer is more dangerous because of its ability to spread to other organs more rapidly if it is not treated at an early stage.

  • Appears as an irregular mole with multiple colors (brown, black, red, or blue).
  • Can develop anywhere on the body, including areas not exposed to the sun.
  • Early detection and treatment are crucial.

Symptoms of Skin Cancer

  • New or changing moles.
  • Sores that do not heal.
  • Red, scaly patches of skin.
  • Unusual lumps or bumps on the skin.
  • Itchy or bleeding lesions.

Risk Factors

  • Excessive exposure to UV radiation.
  • Fair skin, light hair, and freckles.
  • Weakened immune system.
  • Family history of skin cancer.
  • History of sunburns, especially in childhood.

How to Prevent Skin Cancer

  • Apply sunscreen with SPF 30+ every day.
  • Wear protective clothing, hats, and sunglasses.
  • Avoid tanning beds and prolonged sun exposure.
  • Regularly check your skin for new or changing spots.
  • See a dermatologist for regular check-ups.

Treatment Options

  • Surgery: Removing the affected area is the most common treatment.
  • Radiation Therapy: Used for cancers that are difficult to remove surgically.
  • Chemotherapy: Topical or systemic treatment for advanced skin cancers.
  • Immunotherapy: Boosts the body's immune system to fight cancer.
  • Targeted Therapy: Uses drugs to attack specific cancer cells.

Other information on Skin Cancer

Statistics and Facts
  • Mortality:Approximately 2,000 Australians die from skin cancer each year, with melanoma being a significant cause of death.
  • Incidence:In 2023, around 18,257 new cases of melanoma were diagnosed.
  • Trends:The incidence of melanoma has increased over time, but the age-specific incidence rate in those under 40 has decreased.
  • Risk Factors:Exposure to UV radiation from the sun is the primary cause of skin cancer, and lighter skin color increases the risk of melanoma.
The UV index

The UV Index is a scale that measures the intensity of ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the sun, indicating the risk of sunburn and long-term skin damage. It ranges from 0 to 11+. Generally, a UV Index of 3 or higher warrants sun protection measures.

  • 0-2:Low danger, minimal protection needed.
  • 3-5:Moderate danger, wear a shirt, sunglasses, hat, and sunscreen.
  • 6-7:High danger, seek shade, and use all sun protection methods.
  • 8-10:Very high danger, avoid being outside during midday hours, seek shade, and use all sun protection measures.
  • 11+:Extreme danger, avoid being outside during midday hours, seek shade, and use all sun protection measures.
